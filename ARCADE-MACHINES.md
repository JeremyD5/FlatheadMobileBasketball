# Arcade Machines

Four shooting stations. Three sourcing routes, priced separately.

**Status:** exploratory. Prices are estimates as of September 2026.

---

## What you're actually buying

A commercial arcade basketball machine is six subsystems:

1. **Backboard panel** — printed or painted, usually acrylic or alupanel
2. **Rim + net** with an IR or photoelectric **scoring sensor** at the hoop
3. **LED scoreboard** — 7-segment or dot-matrix, shows score and countdown
4. **Control PCB** — game logic, timer, sound, coin/free-play, sometimes links units for head-to-head
5. **Amp + speaker** — announcer audio, buzzer, escalating music
6. **Cabinet** — steel tube frame, cage mesh, and the sloped ball return

Some models add a **motor that moves the hoop side to side** at high scores. Bay Tek's [Super Shot](https://primetimeamusements.com/product/super-shot/) moved the whole backboard front-to-back instead, for distance rather than lateral difficulty.

### The key insight

**The scoring electronics are ~10% of the cost and ~10% of the difficulty. The cabinet and ball return are the other 90%.**

And critically: **we are not building four freestanding machines. We're building four stations into a trailer.** The trailer wall is the backboard mount. The trailer floor is the ball return. The lane rails are welded tube with mesh spanning between them.

A commercial machine is 93" deep mostly because it has to carry its own ball return and stand up unsupported. Ours doesn't. That's why Route C is viable here in a way it wouldn't be for someone building a standalone machine.

### Reference dimensions (US-stocked equivalent)

| Spec | Value |
|---|---|
| Width | 41" |
| Depth | 93" |
| Height | 109" (95" without top guard) |
| Weight | ~650 lb |

Four across = ~164" (13.7 ft) of a 16 ft box. **Height is the constraint** — a stock 7 ft trailer ceiling will not clear a 95–109" machine, so either order/find a trailer with extra height, cut the machines down, or build to the ceiling from the start (Route C).

**Duty note:** most manufactured Chinese goods currently stack to roughly 37.5% — MFN base plus Section 301 — plus a $100–250 broker entry fee. **Verify current rates before ordering**, this changes.

### Pros
- Complete, working machines with proper game logic, audio, and moving-hoop mechanism
- Custom backboard graphics from the factory
- 1-year warranty on core components from most suppliers
- MOQ is usually 1, and samples ship in 7–15 days

### Cons
- 10–14 week total lead time. **A spring launch means ordering by December.**
- Freight and duty roughly double the machine cost
- Repairs mean emailing Guangzhou mid-season
- You're still cutting them down to fit the ceiling
- Tariff exposure — the rate could move between order and arrival

### Before ordering
- Request a sample unit first (refundable against the full order at most suppliers)
- Ask for SGS or BV audit reports
- Confirm 110V, 50/60Hz
- Use Alibaba Trade Assurance — claims within 30 days of receipt
- Get the crated dimensions in CBM before quoting freight

## Route C — Electronics from China, structure built here

**Cost: $3,000–6,500 · Lead time: 4–8 weeks · Effort: high**

**Recommended.** Buy the scoring/game electronics as a parts kit from the same Chinese factories, fabricate the frames, lane rails, and ball return locally to fit the trailer exactly.

### Why this is the best fit

- **No cabinet needed.** The trailer is the cabinet.
- **Ball return is a sloped plywood floor** you're building anyway.
- **You design to your ceiling height from day one**, and you pick the rim height. Slightly lower plays better for the 6–10 crowd, who are the core customers.
- **Repairs are a $10 sensor from a drawer of spares**, not a mystery PCB from overseas.
- **No coin mech needed** — free play only.
- **Electronics ship air freight in days**, not on a boat. Duty on a $400–800 order is noise.
- **Zero IP exposure.** Nothing resembles anyone's product.

### Option C1 — Chinese parts kit (preferred)

Buy the PCB + sensor + LED display + speaker as a bundle. Alibaba explicitly lists "Coin Operated Basketball Arcade Game Machine Kit PCB DIY Bundles Parts Kit."

- [Alibaba — arcade basketball machine parts](https://www.alibaba.com/showroom/arcade-basketball-game-machine-parts.html)

**~$80–200 per station, $400–800 for all four.**

You get proven arcade game logic and — importantly — the announcer voice, buzzer, and escalating music. That audio is a real share of why the machine is fun and it's the hardest part to replicate yourself.

Ask suppliers about: retrofit kits (Xinqi and Flying Tech are mentioned as offering these), and whether the kit supports linking multiple units for head-to-head play.

## What's genuinely hard about Route C

Being honest about this before committing:

### 1. Reliability, not electronics
Commercial IR scoring claims ~98% shot registration. A missed basket at a birthday party is a complaint, and four kids shooting simultaneously means four chances per second to get it wrong.

**Mitigation:** two break beams per rim with debounce logic. Budget real time for tuning.

### 2. Game feel
The announcer voice, the buzzer, the music that speeds up as the clock runs down — that's a large share of why the machine is fun and it's the part you can't fabricate. This is the strongest argument for buying the Chinese parts kit (C1) over building from scratch (C2).

**Do not use NBA or other licensed audio.** Source royalty-free or record your own.

### 3. The moving hoop
Idaho Falls' trailer has hoops that slide side to side once a player hits a score threshold. It's a genuine wow feature.

- Linear actuator + limit switches: ~$80–150 per station
- **But** it's a moving part on a rig that vibrates down Highway 93 all summer
- **Recommendation:** skip it for v1. Add later if customers ask.

### 4. Time
Figure **60–120 hours** for a first-time builder to get four stations working reliably. They need to be done and shaken out well before the first booking, not the week of.

### 5. No warranty
Chinese complete machines ship with a 1-year warranty and lifetime after-sales support. A Route C build ships with you.

**Mitigation:** buy 2× spares of every electronic component up front. At these prices a full spare kit is ~$200.

## Recommendation

**Route C1** — Chinese parts kit, structure fabricated locally.

Best cost, no ocean freight, designed to the actual trailer, cheap and fast repairs, no IP exposure, and you keep the arcade audio and game logic that make it feel like a real machine.

**Fallback if the build stalls:** Route A with four refurbished used machines. More expensive but fast, and you can decide that in March if the fabrication isn't coming together.

## Open questions

- [ ] Contact 2–3 Alibaba suppliers and request quotes on parts kits only (not complete machines)
- [ ] Ask specifically: does the kit support linking units for head-to-head play?
- [ ] Order one sample kit and prototype a single station before committing to four
- [ ] Confirm trailer interior height, then set rim height and shooting distance
- [ ] Decide: 32×64 LED matrix (more flexible, animations) vs. simple 7-segment (cheaper, more legible in sunlight)
- [ ] **Sunlight legibility** — these run outdoors in Montana summer. Test display brightness before buying four.
- [ ] Find a local welder and get a quote on lane rails + frames
- [ ] Source royalty-free arcade audio, or confirm the Chinese kit's audio is acceptable
- [ ] Cold-weather testing if pursuing winter indoor bookings — LCD/LED behavior below freezing during transport
