# Kee Klamp BOM — Angled Lane Dividers

Metal only, upper see-through zone. Angled top rail matching the commercial arcade machine profile.

**Prices from [Simplified Building](https://www.simplifiedbuilding.com/pipe-fittings/kee-klamp), September 10, 2026.**

---

## ⚠️ Correction to an earlier estimate

An earlier note in this repo estimated Kee Klamp at **$1,500–2,500** for five panels. **That was wrong, and the angled design is exactly why.**

Kee Klamp's fixed fittings are $22–70. Its **adjustable-angle** fittings — the ones required to land a sloped top rail on a vertical post — run **$56 to $142 each**. The angled design needs three per panel, fifteen across the build. That's roughly **$1,425 in adjustable fittings alone.**

Corrected total below.

## Panel geometry

Shared dividers: 4 lanes need 5 panels (3 interior + 2 ends), or 3 if the trailer front and rear walls close the ends.

| Member | Length |
|---|---|

| Bottom rail | 93" |
| Angled top rail (√(93² + 30²)) | 98" |
| **Pipe per panel** | **362" ≈ 30.2 ft** |

Pipe size: **Kee size 7** (1.660" OD, 1-1/4" nominal). Standard structural railing size.

## Pipe and mesh

| Item | Detail | Cost/panel |
|---|---|---|
| Sch 40 galvanized pipe, 1-1/4" | 30.2 ft @ ~$6/ft | **$181** |
| Chain link fabric + hardware | Share of one 6' × 50' 9-ga roll (~$200–280) plus tension bars, brace bands, hog rings (~$150–250) | **~$90** |

**Kee Klamp has no slot to capture a rigid mesh panel.** That's a real design consequence: you can't drop a welded wire sheet into pipe the way you can into 80/20 extrusion. Options are chain link fabric with tension bars and brace bands (standard fence practice, cheap, what's specced here), hog-ringing a welded panel to the pipe (crude, how dog kennels are built), or welding a sub-frame around the mesh, which defeats the point of buying no-weld fittings.

## Head to head

| | Kee Klamp | [80/20](8020-BOM.md) | Chain link fence framework |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 panels | **~$4,200** | ~$3,950 | **~$1,470** |
| Weight | ~545 lb | ~350 lb | ~500 lb |
| Angled joints | Adjustable fittings, $95 ea | Mitercut, $5.31 ea | Miter + rail-end cup, ~$4 |
| Mesh capture | None — lash or tension it | Drops into T-slot | Tension bar, standard |
| Tools | Hex key, pipe cutter | Hex key, chop saw | Wrench, pipe cutter |
| Reconfigurable | Yes, fully | Yes, fully | Partly |

**Kee Klamp is the most expensive option and the second heaviest.** It is genuinely excellent hardware — galvanized, engineered to defined load standards, assembles with a hex key, lasts 30+ years — but it's priced for OSHA guardrail contracts, not for a five-panel one-off.

### Chain link fence framework, angled version

| Item | Per panel |
|---|---|
| 1-3/8" galvanized top rail, ~30 ft @ ~$2.20/ft | $66 |
| Terminal posts ×3 @ ~$30 | $90 |
| Rail end cups, brace bands, post caps, tension bars (~12 pcs) | ~$48 |
| Chain link fabric share | ~$90 |
| **Per panel** | **~$294** |
| **5 panels** | **~$1,470** |

Angled joints are handled by mitering the rail and using a rail-end cup with a brace band. Cruder than an adjustable Kee fitting, and not repositionable, but it's how every ball field backstop in the valley is built and it costs a third as much.

---

## Recommendation

**Fence framework for v1.** Get the angle, the lane width, and the rim height right at $1,470 before spending $4,200 on hardware that locks in dimensions you haven't validated.

If the prototype proves out and the finished look matters for pricing, 80/20 is the better upgrade than Kee: cheaper, 200 lb lighter, and the T-slot solves the mesh capture problem that Kee doesn't.
